# You Me Everybody Cow Come

Convert a photograph into the user's established **fusion style**. The source supplies composition, pose, place skeleton, and identity anchors. Cow Come supplies the cow-human species, primitive reconstruction, rough matte surfaces, missing information, impossible color, and badly composited early-3D feeling.

This is not a literal film imitation and not a generic cow mascot.

## Highest-priority calibration

These accepted outputs are the visual authority and override older baseline images and conflicting rules:

- `assets/fusion-target-orange-seated.png`
- `assets/fusion-target-yellow-gesture.png`

The written specification preserves the diagnostic conclusions from their source-to-output comparison. The original source photographs are not required during generation.

Read `references/style-spec.md` for the reverse-engineered construction, scene-reduction, palette, and lighting rules. Older assets may provide local motifs only; they must not restore the former fixed body template.

## Intake

Before a new user's first generation, ask only the missing choices below, together in one compact message. Do not conduct a longer interview:

1. **Output ratio:** `keep the source ratio (recommended)` / `landscape 4:3` / `portrait 3:4` / `square 1:1` / `another ratio`.
2. **Color creativity:** `restrained` / `balanced hybrid (recommended and default)` / `bold`.

Use this exact user-facing structure, translated into the user's language when necessary:

> 生成前确认两个选择：  
> 1）成图比例：保持原图比例（推荐）／横图 4:3／竖图 3:4／正方形 1:1／其他比例；  
> 2）色彩创意程度：克制／平衡混合（推荐、默认）／大胆。

If the user already supplied either answer, ask only for the missing one. If both are supplied, generate immediately. If the user says “you decide,” “use the default,” or “generate directly,” use the source ratio and **balanced hybrid** without blocking. For a batch, one pair of answers applies to the whole batch unless the user assigns per-image overrides. Do not repeat these questions within the same active request after they have been answered.

Map `restrained` to source-led color, `balanced hybrid` to balanced-hybrid color, and `bold` to creative-led color. Treat hide color, exact palette family, creative-field location, sky method, abstraction, and scene pruning as hidden skill decisions unless the user overrides them.

For a batch, deliberately vary hide colors, color strategy, the location of the creative color field, and sky treatment. Do not merely rotate the same lavender-mint-coral palette through every frame.

## Analyze the source

Silently extract:

- subject count, broad placement, scale, pose mechanics, gesture, head direction, and visible-body range;
- camera height, horizon, major negative space, depth layers, and left/right visual weight;
- exact hair silhouette and length, glasses, brow shape, gaze direction, and one extra identity cue;
- two to four source palette anchors drawn from identity- or place-defining areas such as clothing, hair/fur, roofs, plants, furniture, wood, or a memorable object;
- dominant clothing color, broad garment silhouette, and at most one to three large identity-defining color patches;
- pose-critical furniture and held objects;
- three to six landmark groups that alone can identify the place after most detail is deleted.

Treat every extracted material cue as information to reinterpret, never as pixels or surface appearance to preserve. The source may define an object's identity, count, broad silhouette, placement, color family, and function; it does not authorize retaining photographic fur, eyes, skin, plants, ceramic, glass, metal, fabric, soil, food, screens, or reflections.

Ignore jewelry, logos, garment construction, denim treatment, seams, pockets, waistbands, folds, wrinkles, fabric drape, incidental tableware, small architecture, foliage detail, repeated trim, and secondary clutter.

## Composition translation

Preserve the source as a recognizable spatial skeleton rather than a detailed reconstruction.

- Keep the subject in the same broad quadrant, at comparable visual scale, with the same pose and camera height.
- Preserve the negative-space pattern and key relationships among subject, supporting furniture, architecture, and vegetation.
- When ratio changes, recompose the broad masses intentionally. Crop or shift secondary information, but retain the source's visual logic.
- Preserve the visible body range unless the requested ratio requires a modest crop. Never invent a standing full body from a portrait.
- Preserve only three to six source landmark groups; delete or merge most information inside and between them.

The result should be traceable to the source while looking radically stripped down.

## Universal source-material reconstruction gate

Every visible subject and object must be rebuilt in the same authored Cow Come world. This applies equally to people, animals, plants, props, furniture, architecture, screens, food, and close-up hero objects.

- Never cut out, transplant, preserve, or locally overpaint a photographic source region. Adding roughness, posterization, or a clay filter to an otherwise source-faithful object is not enough.
- Preserve only identity-bearing structure: broad silhouette, count, placement, pose/function, dominant color family, and one or two recognition cues. Redesign exact contours, proportions, surface breaks, small features, and construction as crude primitives.
- Share one low-frequency handmade surface vocabulary across foreground and background. No isolated realistic island may survive inside a simplified frame.
- Animals become blunt lumpy sculptures. Fur collapses into one or a few matte masses; eyes become flat inserted ovals/discs with matte pupils and no iris detail, catchlights, wetness, glass depth, lashes, or photographic reflection.
- Plants and flowers become a few paddles, discs, rods, or blunt petal lumps. Remove photographic leaf veins, serration, translucency, soil, gravel, and sharp botanical tips.
- Hero objects such as planters, cups, toys, food, screens, and appliances must be visibly resculpted: simplify and slightly alter their exact silhouette and proportions, expose crude joins, and rebuild markings as uneven daubs or grooves. Do not keep a precise product contour with only a new texture.
- Glass and metal become opaque or weakly translucent flat masses with at most one crude highlight patch. Fabric becomes a painted slab or shell. Screens become color fields or blunt shapes rather than retained UI or photographic imagery.
- Any selective low-resolution texture must be regenerated or generic to the new scene. Never lift an unchanged source texture patch into the output.

If any region still looks plausibly cropped from the photograph, resculpt that region before delivery.

## People → fused Cow Come characters

Replace every person with a plainly bovine upright cow-human while preserving the original pose and presence.

- Preserve identity through hair silhouette, glasses, brows, gaze, pose, clothing color, and one or two memorable prop cues.
- Use a broad simple cow head, sideways leaf ears, two short uneven horns, flat inserted eyes, and a separately attached pale lavender/pink-gray muzzle. It must read as a cow-human, never a human wearing horns.
- Follow the source body's seated, leaning, reaching, crossed-leg, or standing structure. Simplify it into a few thick, lumpy beginner-sculpted masses. Do not force every source into one long-trunk, ultra-short-leg child template.
- Keep large pose-defining masses: seated thighs, bent knees, reaching arms, hands around a phone, or a torso leaning against a chair.
- Clothing becomes one broad thick matte shell or painted body field using the source's dominant color. Treat tops, skirts, shorts, and trousers as simple silhouette masses, not simulated fabric. It may merge awkwardly into the torso or legs.
- Give clothing a naive graffiti-painted finish: one flat base color, optionally with one to three oversized uneven hand-daubed patches or blunt doodle shapes when the source pattern matters. Keep boundaries crude and low-detail. Do not reproduce denim wash, jean wrinkles, folds, seams, stitching, pockets, waistbands, belt loops, ribbing, weave, realistic drape, garment construction, logos, or small repeated prints.
- Make visible limbs slightly uneven and under-resolved, but retain the gesture. Hands are deliberately small, simplified, and cutely symbolic: a chalk-white/pale mitten or glove mass with three to four short rounded partly fused digits. Preserve only the minimum silhouette needed to read pointing, holding, or gripping. For two hands gripping one object, use two small full glove-like hands rather than realistic fingers. Never model knuckles, joints, nails, tendons, palm lines, skin folds, or human hand anatomy. Fully hide-colored or anatomically human hands are a failure.
- Preserve a held phone, cup, bag, or other object that explains the hand pose, using one blunt recognizable primitive.
- Hair is a coarse solid shell with a few blunt grooves. Preserve fringe, part, side volume, and endpoint length.
- Eyes are flat white ovals or soft almonds with equal matte dark pupils. Preserve the source gaze. No realistic sockets, eyelids, irises, highlights, wetness, or lashes.
- Use a compact pale lavender/pink-gray muzzle occupying roughly one quarter to one third of the face. Build it as a blunt rounded rectangle or low oval, not a protruding pig snout.
- Form the nose as two shallow elongated slot-like dents pressed into the upper half of the muzzle. They may be short vertical, diagonal, or soft almond grooves, but must stay low-contrast and material-colored. Never use two black bean dots, round button holes, beads, or a separate pig nose pad.
- A mouth is mandatory on every cow-human: add one clearly visible separate straight or slightly uneven horizontal seam across the lower third of the muzzle, below and distinct from the nostril dents. Closed is fine; missing mouth, mouth merged into a nostril, human lips, or a pig-like two-hole snout is a failure.
- Choose bright warm yellow, golden yellow, vivid orange, or orange-red hide. Do not derive hide from human skin.

Aim for awkward sincerity rather than maximum ugliness. Cute color is welcome; cute mascot anatomy is not.

## Radical scene reduction

Delete roughly 60–80% of the source environment before rebuilding it.

- Keep only three to six landmark groups necessary for place recognition, pose support, and composition.
- Convert buildings to boxes and a few roof slabs; umbrellas to one canopy plus one pole; chairs to rods and planes; planters to one box; plants to a few stems, broad discs, or one hedge mass; phones to one rectangle.
- Retain a repeated chair or roof rhythm only when it defines the place, and reduce it to a few crude representatives.
- Keep pose-supporting furniture and held objects. Do not remove the chair beneath a seated person or the phone explaining joined hands.
- Merge layered roofs into a few gray blocks, detailed plant beds into one box plus sparse discs, and dense grass into one crude mass or several bent strips.
- Large empty fields and missing information are positive. Do not replace the source with an unrelated preset landscape.

## Hybrid source-anchored color and optional creative field

Cute color remains a first-class capability, but it is an accent system rather than a compulsory full-scene skin. Preserve two to four source palette anchors after materially rebuilding the scene. Color may survive from the photograph; photographic surface, light, and texture may not.

Use the user's selected color strategy. When the user has not selected one, always start from **balanced hybrid**, regardless of whether the source is a portrait, animal, object, interior, or place:

- **Source-led — the `restrained` choice:** let roughly 60–80% of the large color masses follow transformed source anchors. Reserve candy color, cloud color, or tie-dye for one or two zones occupying roughly 20–40% of the frame.
- **Balanced hybrid — recommended and universal default:** preserve recognizable clothing, hair/fur, vegetation, roof, furniture, wood, object, or neutral families, then give one large field and optionally one tiny echo a complementary invented color treatment. The invented treatment may be vivid, but it must not homogenize the scene.
- **Creative-led — the `bold` choice or an explicit request for stronger invention:** allow one suitable large blank sky, wall, window, screen, rug, floor patch, or backdrop to dominate while retaining at least two or three source anchors and one dirty neutral.

Possible invented accents include candy cyan, butter yellow, coral, dusty pink, lavender, mint, teal, apricot, electric blue, faded magenta, sulfur yellow, cream, and moss green. Prefer dusty or dirty versions when the source is subdued. Keep gray, brown, dark green, charcoal, off-white, or pale wood when they help the place remain itself.

Use a creative field selectively:

- choose at most one large field such as sky, window, wall, screen, rug, floor patch, or distant backdrop;
- render it as one flat color, one badly mapped low-resolution cloud plate, or one crude two-to-four-color tie-dye/轧染 field;
- permit at most one small echo elsewhere; do not repeat the same cloud/tie-dye treatment across multiple independent surfaces;
- do not recolor every wall, object, plant, roof, floor, and garment into one pastel family.

Across a batch, rotate the strategy and the creative-field location, not only the hues. Avoid repeating full-room pastelization, the same candy triad, or the same distribution of lavender, mint, coral, and yellow. Source weather and exposure never control lighting, but a source sky color may be retained as an anchor, muted, shifted, or replaced. Never build modeled fluffy clouds.

## Materials and disconnected light

- Use dry matte surfaces with broad bumpy displacement, shallow dents, irregular edges, and obvious primitive joins.
- Keep texture low-frequency. Avoid dense pores, realistic fur, fabric weave, fine bark, and photoreal surface detail.
- Apply stretched low-resolution texture selectively to roofs, gray blocks, foliage, poles, or the sky plate. Leave large neighboring areas nearly flat.
- Reject one coherent global light direction. Give each major object only cheap local diffuse/form shading, with brightness and direction allowed to disagree.
- The sky never relights the scene.
- Disable cast, projected, receiving, contact, and ground shadows. No ambient occlusion, contact darkening, bounce light, color bleeding, or shared physical relationship between separate objects.
- Keep enough dull self-shading inside each object to show its volume. The result should feel like separately previewed assets hard-composited together.
- No glossy PBR, wetness, wax, polished clay, cinematic contrast, rim light, atmosphere, or depth of field.
- Clothes must read as painted color masses on a crude body shell, never as carefully sculpted or realistically textured garments.

## Generate

Use image generation as an edit of the user's photograph. Include the user source plus both authoritative target outputs whenever multiple local references are supported. Label their roles in the prompt:

1. user photograph controls people, identity, pose, crop logic, composition skeleton, and retained place landmarks;
2. target outputs control cow species, sculpt language, material roughness, scene pruning, palette behavior, sky randomization, and disconnected render finish;
3. never copy a target's person, pose, props, or layout into another source.

State the requested ratio and source-specific anchors. Generate one image at a time unless the user requests a batch.

Never reproduce text, watermarks, phone UI, reference borders, or image labels.

## Self-check

Regenerate if:

- the output follows a generic film still or unrelated environment more than the source composition;
- the source pose, quadrant, visible-body range, gaze, hair, glasses, clothing color, or pose-critical prop is lost;
- every source is forced into the old fixed child-body silhouette;
- the character becomes a human with cow accessories, realistic cattle, a plush toy, or polished mascot;
- two to four source palette anchors disappear, or the frame mechanically copies every source color without any authored intervention;
- more than half of unrelated scene objects are recolored candy without a source or compositional reason, every surface shares one high-saturation pastel family, or the scene reads like a homogeneous palette swap;
- cloud/tie-dye treatment repeats across several independent surfaces instead of occupying one deliberate large field plus at most one small echo;
- modeled clouds replace a flat, cloud-plate, or tie-dye field, or the sky restores source lighting coherence;
- the muzzle becomes huge, pillowy, horse-like, glossy, or too small to read;
- the muzzle becomes a pig snout, uses two dark bean-dot/button nostrils, lacks a separate visible horizontal mouth seam, or merges nose and mouth into one mark;
- any animal, plant, prop, hero object, fabric, glass, metal, screen, eye, fur, soil, or other region retains photographic source material, precise source contour, realistic micro-detail, or cutout-like edge treatment instead of being visibly rebuilt from the shared crude primitive vocabulary;
- more than roughly 40% of environmental detail survives, or the place becomes totally unrecognizable;
- held objects or supporting furniture disappear and the pose stops making sense;
- clothing develops denim wash, jean creases, seams, pockets, waistbands, fabric weave, realistic drape, or many small pattern details instead of a simple graffiti-painted shell;
- hands become fully hide-colored, anatomically human, large and dominant, finely articulated, or show knuckles, joints, nails, tendons, palm lines, or skin folds instead of the intended small pale mitten symbols;
- separate objects share coherent global light, cast or receive shadows, contact-darken one another, or look physically integrated;
- the frame becomes glossy, cinematic, photoreal, clean low-poly, premium clay animation, or clean children's illustration;
- color cuteness spreads into rounded mascot anatomy, friendly plush volume, or exaggerated kawaii facial expression.

Deliver the image without exposing hidden analysis.
